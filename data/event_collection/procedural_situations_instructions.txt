SITUATION TAGGING (Virtual Villagers island events; procedural mixer)

You READ each event and tag it. Purpose: an answer (A) with its outcomes (O) may be offered under ANOTHER event's
setup when the situations fit, so the mixed event still makes sense. Judge as a careful story editor: would a player
reading setup X, then this answer and outcome, find it natural? When in doubt, say it does NOT move.

Each batch file lists events:
  ### E <event id>
  T: title          S: setup text (the situation posed)
  A <id>: <button label> || <optional text shown after the click>
     O <id>: <outcome text>          (only answers/outcomes that could move at all are listed)

FIXED VOCABULARY (use only these words)

SITUATIONS (what a setup poses / what an answer can respond to):
  newcomer       a visitor, stranger, traveler or newcomer is present or arrives
  sickness       someone is ill, hurt, tired, recovering or needs care
  food_shortage  food is scarce, spoiled, lost or must be shared out
  lost_object    something is missing, lost, broken or misplaced
  quarrel        villagers disagree, argue, compete for something, or one is upset with another
  strange_sign   something unexplained, eerie, an omen, a mystery
  celebration    a festival, feast, gathering, gift-giving or happy occasion
  child_problem  a child's worry, question, behaviour or need
  elder_wish     an elder's need, wish, memory, decline or advice
  craft_problem  making, building, repairing, a tool or a craft goes wrong or needs a decision
  sea_danger     danger or trouble at sea, the reef, a boat, the shore
  danger         other physical danger: a fall, fire, a dangerous place or creature
  grief          a death, a loss, mourning, missing someone
  fear           someone is afraid, anxious, nervous or overwhelmed
  new_idea       an invention, experiment, discovery, question about how things work, learning a skill
  animal         an animal encounter, a pet, wildlife behaving oddly
  weather        storm, heat, rain, drought, a season's trouble
  personal_change someone wants to change their look, name, role, habits or life path
  feelings       embarrassment, loneliness, jealousy, pride, hurt feelings, self-doubt
  leadership     a rule, a decision for the village, authority, duties, fairness
  tradition      a custom, ritual, belief, ancestors, the spirits
  kindness       a favour, a gift, gratitude, helping someone, a secret good deed
  contest        a game, contest, challenge or performance
  farming        gardens, crops, fruit trees, harvest
  art_story      music, song, story, dance, painting, carving as art
  secret         a secret, a confession, a hidden truth
  mistake        someone made a mistake or caused an accident

NEEDS (what must already be in the scene for an answer/outcome to make sense; list only what it truly relies on):
  healer child elder food sea animal visitor tools plant fire music chief group
  ("group" = several villagers together; "chief" = the village leader; leave empty when it relies on nothing)

TONE (one): gentle practical bold funny solemn

WHAT TO WRITE: one line per item, nothing else, pipe-separated:
  S|<setup piece id = event id + "_s">|<1-3 situations, comma>|<needs, comma or empty>|<tone>
  A|<answer id>|<1-4 situations it can sensibly answer, comma>|<needs>|<tone>|<y or n>
  O|<outcome id>|<needs>|<y or n>

y/n = MAY MOVE to another event's setup that poses one of its situations:
  - A: "y" only if the button makes sense on its own for ANY setup with those situations (e.g. "Ask an Elder for
    Advice", "Comfort the Child", "Share the Remaining Food", "Welcome the Visitor With a Meal"). "n" if it names or
    assumes a particular thing of its own scene ("Inspect the Rope", "Sketch the Fish", "Keep the New Color"), or
    refers to "it/this/that" from its own scene.
  - O: "y" only if the outcome reads naturally after its answer in any setup with the answer's situations
    ("The child calms down and falls asleep.", "The visitor thanks them and stays the night."). "n" if it continues
    its own scene's particular story (named objects, earlier events, specific places, "the rope", "the crab").
  - An answer that moves needs at least one outcome with y, else write n.
  - Never y for romance, punishment, or anything that decides over someone else's body or life.
  - Most answers and outcomes are n. That is fine. Be strict but not stingy: generic, kind, practical answers that
    fit their situations SHOULD be y.

Write S lines for EVERY event (even ones with no A lines). Use the ids exactly as given.
