NEW EVENTS - TREE OF LIFE SUPPLIES (SUP-01..14, draft for the owner, 2026-11-17)
Cutting tools, bars of soap and bolts of cloth in The Tree of Life: blades made, lost and found, soap that slips away or smells of flowers, and sacks for the yellow fruit. Funny, practical and warm.
Written in the style of the owner's own events. Edit anything and send it back; nothing ships until you approve.
(name1)/(name2)/(name3) fill in with villagers' names; (he/she), (him/her), (his/her) follow the villager.
All Isola rules apply. The Tree of Life only. Each [effect: ...] really changes the village's cutting tools, soap or cloth bolts (a sack is a cloth bolt), and only once the village has invented that item; before that the words are story only.

SUP-01. The Blackberry Blade Contest (VV4 only)
(picks two adults and an elder)

(name1) and (name2) have each sharpened a cutting tool from a fish bone, and each one says theirs is the best on Isola. They have argued about it since breakfast. (name3), who is old enough to have heard every argument there is, tells them to settle it at the blackberry bushes: whoever fills a basket first wins.

Race at the Bushes:
They race. Thorns fly, berries fly, and so does a good deal of language (name3) pretends not to hear. Halfway through, (name1)'s tool snaps on a thick stem with a sad little click. (name2) wins by a handful and is unbearable about it until dinner, when (he/she) quietly offers (name1) half the berries. [effect: the village loses 1 cutting tool]

Let the Elder Judge the Edges:
(name3) tests both tools on a leaf, then on a vine, then on a very old piece of dried fish. "Both excellent," (he/she) announces, and drops them both in the village's tool basket. "Good tools belong to everyone." (name1) and (name2) look at each other, outsmarted, and start sharpening two more. [effect: the village gains 2 cutting tools]

SUP-02. The Soap That Smelled of Flowers (VV4 only)
(picks a child aged 6-13, an adult and a grandmother)

(name1) has been watching (name2) make soap all morning, and while nobody was looking, (he/she) dropped a handful of white flower petals into the pot. Now the whole clearing smells sweet. (name2) is holding up the first bar, sniffing it, trying to decide whether to be cross.

Make a Whole Batch:
(name2) decides not to be cross. (He/she) sends (name1) for more petals, and they make a whole batch together. For weeks, everyone in the village smells faintly of flowers, and the bees follow the washers home. (name1) tells everyone it was (his/her) idea, which is true. [effect: the village gains 3 bars of soap]

Keep the One Bar as a Gift:
(name2) wraps the single sweet bar in a leaf and tells (name1) to give it to someone who needs cheering up. (name1) thinks hard and gives it to (name3), the oldest grandmother in the village, who sniffs it, laughs, and says it smells like the year she was married. [effect: the village gains 1 bar of soap]

SUP-03. Bubbles in the Stream (VV4 only)
(picks an adult and a child aged 5-12)

(name1) is washing at the stream with (name2) when the bar of soap shoots out of (his/her) wet hands like a fish and lands in the water. It bobs away downstream, leaving a trail of bubbles. (name2) is already running after it, shrieking with delight.

Chase It Downstream:
They chase it past the rocks, past the bend, past two very surprised fishers. (name2) finally scoops it out of the pool at the bottom, holding it up like a trophy. It is a little smaller than it was. Both of them are soaked to the waist. They walk home dripping, very pleased with themselves.

Let the Stream Have It:
"Let it go," says (name1), laughing. They sit on the bank and watch the bubbles drift away toward the sea, until the last one pops. (name2) says the fish will be the cleanest fish on Isola. (name1) says that's the most useful thing anyone has done all day. [effect: the village loses 1 bar of soap]

SUP-04. A Sack for the Yellow Fruit (VV4 only)
(picks two adults)

(name1) has been carrying yellow fruit back from the trees in (his/her) arms, and has dropped more than (he/she) has carried. There is a trail of fruit all the way from the grove. (name2) watches (him/her) chase one down the slope and says, "You know we have cloth, don't you?"

Make a Sack From a Bolt of Cloth:
(name2) fetches a bolt of cloth from the store, and together they fold and tie it into a good, deep sack. (name1) fills it in no time and carries it home over one shoulder, whistling. (He/she) goes back for the fruit on the slope as well. Nothing is wasted. [effect: the village loses 1 cloth bolt; small food gain]

Carry Them the Old Way:
(name1) says (his/her) arms are perfectly good, thank you. It takes eleven trips. On the twelfth, (name2) walks beside (him/her) with a basket on each hip and doesn't say a word, which is somehow worse than saying anything.

SUP-05. The Bundle on the Tide (VV4 only)
(picks an adult)

At dawn, (name1) finds a bundle on the beach, wrapped tight and tied with a cord of some fiber (he/she) doesn't recognize. Inside is a bolt of cloth, perfectly dry, patterned with little waves in a blue nobody on Isola knows how to make. Where it came from, nobody can say. Some say the sea gives back what it takes. Some say there are other shores.

Dry It and Store It:
(name1) carries it to the store, where everyone comes to look at the blue waves. The weavers argue for days about how the color was made. Nobody works it out. The cloth is strong, and before long it is just one more bolt on the pile, except that people still stop to touch it. [effect: the village gains 1 cloth bolt]

Give It Back to the Sea:
It feels to (name1) like something someone is missing. (He/she) wraps it up again, carries it out past the breakers, and lets the current take it. That night, the wind turns warm and the sea goes very calm. (name1) doesn't know if that means anything. (He/she) sleeps well anyway.

SUP-06. The Pile of Fish Bones (VV4 only)
(picks an adult and a child aged 6-13)

After the biggest catch of the season, there is a mountain of fish bones behind the cooking pit. (name1) is about to throw them out when (he/she) notices a few long, straight ones, just the right shape for a cutting tool. (name2) is already eyeing the rest for something else entirely.

Sharpen the Best Ones:
(name1) picks out the best bones and spends the evening rubbing them to an edge on a flat stone, while (name2) holds the lamp and asks a question roughly every three breaths. By the end, (name2) can tell a good edge from a bad one, and the village has two new cutting tools. [effect: the village gains 2 cutting tools]

Let the Children Make Wind Chimes:
(name2) and the other children string the bones on cords and hang them in the trees. For the rest of the season, every breeze makes the whole village tinkle. Some people find it lovely. Some people find it very, very loud. Everyone agrees it smells a bit fishy for the first few days.

SUP-07. Who Keeps Taking the Soap? (VV4 only)
(picks two adults and a child aged 5-11)

The soap in the store keeps going missing, one bar at a time. (name1) is certain someone is taking it. (name2) is certain (name1) is just counting wrong. They have been arguing about it for three days, and the soap is still going missing.

Set a Watch:
(name1) hides behind the store all night. Just before dawn, a small goat trots up, noses the lid aside, and starts licking a bar of soap with great concentration. (name1) chases it off. The goat has very clean teeth and no regrets. (name2) admits (name1) was right, but only once, and very quietly. [effect: the village loses 1 bar of soap]

Ask Around:
(name1) asks everyone. At last (name3) shuffles forward and confesses: (he/she) has been taking the soap to make bubbles for the babies, because it makes them laugh. (name3) brings back the bar, a good deal smaller than before. Nobody has the heart to be cross. The babies are very clean. [effect: the village loses 1 bar of soap]

SUP-08. The Torn Sail (VV4 only)
(picks two adults)

A gust has torn the sail of the little fishing canoe from top to bottom. (name1) wants it out on the water by tomorrow; the fish are running. (name2) points out that the store has cloth, and that the weavers have been saving it for something important.

Patch It With a Bolt of Cloth:
(name1) decides fish are important. The new patch is bright and stiff and doesn't match anything, and the canoe looks like it is wearing borrowed clothes. It sails beautifully. The weavers sigh and start another bolt. [effect: the village loses 1 cloth bolt]

Weave a Mat Sail Instead:
(name2) shows (name1) how the old ones were made: palm leaves, split and woven tight. It takes three days and a great deal of patience (name1) didn't know (he/she) had. The fish wait. The mat sail is heavier than cloth, but (name1) is very proud of it, and the cloth stays in the store.

SUP-09. The First Bolt (VV4 only)
(picks a teen aged 14-17 and an elder)

(name1) has just finished (his/her) very first bolt of cloth at the clothing hut. It is lumpy. One end is thicker than the other. In one place there is a hole you could put a finger through. (name1) is standing over it, wondering whether to start again before anyone sees.

Put It in the Store With the Rest:
(name2) comes over, looks at it for a long time, and puts it on the pile with the others. "My first one was worse," (he/she) says. "They used it to carry yellow fruit for years. Lumpy cloth still holds fruit." (name1) goes home walking a little taller. [effect: the village gains 1 cloth bolt]

Unpick It and Try Again:
(name1) unpicks the whole thing, thread by thread, and starts over. It takes until sunset. The second one is much better. (name2) watches from the doorway and doesn't say a word, but (he/she) brings (name1) something to eat, and stays until it's done. [effect: the village gains 1 cloth bolt]

SUP-10. Blades Among the Roots (VV4 only)
(picks an adult)

After a stormy night, (name1) finds two cutting tools lying among the roots of the great tree, bound together with a strand of fresh green vine. They are sharp and clean and carry no one's mark. Nobody in the village says they are theirs. Some say the tree is generous to those who care for it. Some say somebody is very forgetful.

Take Them, With Thanks:
(name1) says thank you out loud, to the tree or to whoever is listening, and carries the tools to the basket. They are the best edges anyone has seen in a long time. Nobody ever claims them. (name1) leaves a little water at the roots every morning after that, just in case. [effect: the village gains 2 cutting tools]

Leave Them for Their Owner:
(name1) leaves them where they are. In the morning, they are gone, and the vine with them. Whether their owner came back for them, or the tree took them back, nobody can say. (name1) can't shake the feeling that (he/she) passed some kind of test, though (he/she) has no idea which one.

SUP-11. Too Many Cooks at the Soap Pot (VV4 only)
(picks three adults)

(name1), (name2) and (name3) are making soap together, and each of them has a secret. (name1) has added extra ash. (name2) has added coconut oil. (name3) has added crushed leaves (he/she) won't name. Nobody told the others. The pot is now a strange green, and it smells like a whole garden.

Use the Mixed Batch:
They pour it out anyway. The bars come out green and speckled and very strong. They clean everything. They clean things that were not dirty. (name1) says it was the ash, (name2) says it was the oil, and (name3) just smiles. Nobody will ever be able to make it again. [effect: the village gains 3 bars of soap]

Start Over, One Cook Only:
They draw straws, and (name2) wins. (He/she) makes the next batch alone while the other two sit nearby and offer advice nobody asked for. It comes out perfect. But there is only enough fat left for one bar, so the village gets one perfect bar and a very long story. [effect: the village gains 1 bar of soap]

SUP-12. The Tool Basket in the Stream (VV4 only)
(picks an adult and a child aged 7-13)

On the way to the blackberry bushes, (name1) trips on a root, and the whole basket of cutting tools goes flying into the stream. (name1) sits in the mud, staring at the ripples. (name2), who saw everything, is trying very hard not to laugh.

Dive for Them:
(name1) wades in and feels around on the bottom with (his/her) toes. It is cold and slow and full of things that wriggle. (He/she) finds every tool but one. The last one stays somewhere under the water, probably for good. [effect: the village loses 1 cutting tool]

Ask the Children to Help:
(name2) fetches every child in the village. They line up across the stream like a fishing net and walk it, step by step, shouting each time a toe finds something. They find every single tool, two shells, a lost bead and a very annoyed crab. (name1) thanks them all, one by one.

SUP-13. Washing Day at the Pond (VV4 only)
(picks two adults)

It's washing day, and the whole village is at the pond. (name1) is standing at the edge with the soap, looking at the crowd and at the small stack of bars in the basket, and doing sums in (his/her) head. (name2) wants to know what the holdup is.

Share One Bar Between Everyone:
The bar goes from hand to hand down the line, and everyone uses a little less than they'd like. By the end it is a sliver the size of a fingernail, and the last person in line washes with mostly hope. It is the most laughing anyone has done on washing day in years. [effect: the village loses 1 bar of soap]

Everyone Gets Their Own:
(name2) hands out bar after bar. The pond turns white with foam, the children disappear into it, and the whole village comes home smelling clean. That evening, (name1) looks at the empty basket and sighs. "Worth it," says (name2). [effect: the village loses 2 bars of soap]

SUP-14. Cloth for Blades (VV4 only)
(picks two adults)

The weavers have more cloth than they can store, and the blackberry pickers have hardly any cutting tools left. (name1), who weaves, and (name2), who picks, have been glaring at each other's piles for a week. (name2) finally suggests a trade: one bolt of cloth for a morning of sharpening.

Make the Trade:
(name1) hands over a bolt, and (name2) spends the morning at the sharpening stone. By midday the pickers have new blades and the weavers have a little more room to move. Both of them pretend they got the better deal. Both of them are a little bit right. [effect: the village loses 1 cloth bolt; the village gains 2 cutting tools]

Keep Things as They Are:
They decide everything is fine as it is. The weavers keep tripping over cloth. The pickers keep sharing one blade between three. Every evening (name1) and (name2) look at each other's piles across the fire, and every evening neither of them says anything.
