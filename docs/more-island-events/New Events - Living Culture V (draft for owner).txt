NEW EVENTS - LIVING CULTURE V: FAMILY DRAMA, FEUDS AND LEADERSHIP DISPUTES (LCE-01..50, draft, 2026-11-18)
All sorts of real-life drama suited to Isola: parents with opinions about who their children marry, arranged matches, inheritances, feuds between families, accusations, apologies that are not enough, and arguments about who should lead.
Written in the style of the owner's own events. The owner waived the review ("I'm sure the draft events are fine"); every event passed the Isola rules and the final 15-point review, logged in the notes.
(name1) to (name4) fill in with villagers' names; (he/she), (him/her), (his/her) follow the villager.
All Isola rules apply. All five games unless noted. Marriages and matches are only for those 18 and over, and nobody is ever forced into one. Conflict needs no villain; endings may stay unresolved. A [Solidarity: raise/lower/neutral] tag marks how an ending feels for the village (a planned counter; no effect yet).

LCE-01. The Arranged Match
(picks an adult and an elder)

(name1) is a grown adult, has never met this person, and had no idea any of this was happening. What should (name1) do?

Agree to Meet Them:

(name1) agrees to meet the match, and nothing more. The two of them sit through a very awkward meal while both families watch. Then they go for a walk and discover they both think the whole thing is ridiculous. They become friends. That is all. (name2) is disappointed, and accepts it. [Solidarity: neutral]

Refuse Outright:

(name1) tells (name2) that (he/she) will choose (his/her) own partner, thank you. (name2) is hurt and embarrassed, and has to go back to the other family and apologize. It is a cold season in their hut. Eventually, (name2) admits (he/she) should have asked first. [Solidarity: lower]

(picks two adults)

The fig tree between (name1)'s hut and (name2)'s hut has been the subject of a feud for three generations. Each family says their great-grandparent planted it. This season the tree is heavy with figs, and both families are standing under it with baskets, glaring. What should they do?

(name1) suggests splitting the figs, half each, without deciding who owns the tree. (name2) agrees, suspiciously. They pick together in silence. Halfway through, (name2) passes (name1) a basket without being asked. The feud is not over. But it has slowed down. [effect: small food gain] [Solidarity: raise]

LCE-15. The People's Assembly
(picks two adults)

After the last flood, the chief has asked the village whether to move the huts inland. (name1) wants every single household to speak at the assembly. (name2) points out there are a great many households, and some of them talk for a very long time. How should the assembly be run?

Every Household Speaks:

The assembly takes two full days. Some families want to move. Some will never leave. One family wants to move to a tree. In the end, the village decides to move the huts nearest the water and keep the rest. Nobody is fully satisfied. Everybody was heard. [Solidarity: raise]

Choose Speakers:

Each part of the village chooses one speaker. The assembly takes an afternoon. The decision is the same: move the huts nearest the water. But three families feel nobody spoke for them, and they say so loudly for a month. [Solidarity: lower]
