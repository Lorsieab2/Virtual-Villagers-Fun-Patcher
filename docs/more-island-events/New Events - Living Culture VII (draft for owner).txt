LCG-01. The Weather Readers
(picks an elder and a researcher)

(name1) has walked the island's paths for sixty years and says a storm is coming: the crabs are climbing the trees and the sea smells of iron. (name2) has kept records of the weather for five years and says the signs point to three more calm days. The fishers want to know whether to go out. Who should they trust?

Trust the Old Signs:

The canoes stay home. The storm comes the next afternoon, exactly as (name1) said, and the village is ready for it. (name2) adds the climbing crabs to (his/her) records, and asks (name1) for every other sign (he/she) knows. It takes a month. [effect: small Research skill for (name2)] [Solidarity: raise]

Trust the Records:

The canoes go out. The storm comes a day later than (name1) said and a day sooner than (name2) said, and the crews make it home soaked and frightened. Both of them were partly right. They sit down together and start comparing everything. [effect: small Research skill for (name2); small food gain] [Solidarity: neutral]

LCG-02. The Shared Stores
(picks two adults)

After last year's hungry season, (name1) wants every family to put food into a shared store for emergencies. (name2)'s family is large and barely has enough, while the fishing families have plenty. (name2) says equal shares are not fair shares. How should the store be filled?

Everyone Gives the Same:

Every family gives the same amount. (name2)'s family goes hungry for a week to manage it, and (name2) is quietly furious. When the bad season comes, the store saves everyone, (name2)'s family included. (name2) is glad of it, and still thinks the rule was wrong. [effect: small food loss] [Solidarity: neutral]

Each Gives What They Can:

Each family gives by its means: the fishing families more, the large families less. Some of the fishing families grumble. The store is just as full. When the bad season comes, nobody asks who gave what. [effect: small food loss] [Solidarity: raise]

LCG-03. The Traveling Song
(picks an elder and an adult)

Twenty years ago, (name1) taught the village's farewell song to a young traveler leaving Isola. Today, (name2) has come home from the far islands, singing that same song, with seven new verses about places nobody here has ever seen. (name1) is not sure whether to be honored or upset. What should the village do?

Keep the New Verses:

The village learns all the new verses: about ice that floats, birds that cannot fly, and a fish as long as a canoe. The song is now very long. (name1) adds a verse of (his/her) own, about the song coming home. [Solidarity: raise]

Sing It as It Was:

The village sings it as it always was. (name2) sings the new verses quietly, afterwards, for anyone who wants to hear them. The children want to hear them every night. Before long, everyone knows them anyway. [Solidarity: neutral]

LCG-04. The Path to Water
(picks two adults)

The dry season has been long, and the settlement across the hills has asked to keep using the old path to the village's spring, as their grandparents always did. (name1) says nobody should be refused water. (name2) says the spring is lower than anyone has ever seen it, and the village must come first. What should the village do?

Let Them Come:

The village lets them come, with no conditions. The spring holds, barely. Both villages are thirsty for a month. When the rains return, the settlement sends a canoe full of fish in thanks. [effect: small food gain] [Solidarity: raise]

Set Fair Limits:

(name2) meets with the settlement's elders and they agree: two pots per family, mornings only. Nobody is happy. Nobody goes without. The agreement is carved on a stone by the path, and it is still there long after the rains return. [effect: small Research skill for (name2)] [Solidarity: neutral]

LCG-05. The Terraces and the Three Crops
(picks two farmers)

The old terraces on the hillside are crumbling. (name1) wants to rebuild them stone by stone and plant three crops together the way the old farmers did: beans climbing the stalks, squash leaves shading the soil. (name2) says flat gardens by the stream would be quicker. What should they do?

Rebuild the Terraces:

It takes a whole season to rebuild the walls. The first planting is a tangle of beans, stalks and squash, and (name2) thinks it looks a mess. Then the harvest comes, and it is the best the hillside has given in living memory. [effect: small Farming skill for (name1); small Building skill for (name2)] [Solidarity: raise]

Simpler Gardens by the Stream:

The stream gardens are planted in a month and grow quickly. Then the stream floods and washes half of them away. (name2) helps (name1) start on the terraces after all. [effect: small food loss] [Solidarity: neutral]

LCG-06. The Weaver's Permission
(picks a weaver and an adult)

(name1)'s family has woven the same pattern for six generations: little waves inside diamonds, each wave a family story. A trader from another island wants to copy it on cloth to sell, and does not plan to say where it came from. (name2) thinks the trade would bring the village good tools. It is (name1)'s family's decision. What should (name1) do?

Allow It, With Terms:

(name1) agrees, on terms: only one of the patterns, the trader must say it comes from Isola, and the family gets tools for every bolt sold. The trader grumbles and agrees. The other patterns stay in the family. [effect: small tech gain] [Solidarity: raise]

Refuse:

(name1) refuses. Some patterns are not for sale. The trader is annoyed and leaves. (name2) is disappointed about the tools. (name1)'s grandmother pats (name1) on the cheek and says nothing at all, which in her family means a great deal. [Solidarity: neutral]

LCG-07. The Mountain Council
(picks two adults)

Traders have offered good tools if the village lets them cut wood on the hill whenever they like. (name1) wants the households who use the hill forest to meet and make their own rules first. (name2) says the households have never agreed on anything and the traders' offer is generous. What should the village do?

Make Their Own Rules:

The households meet, argue for four days and agree: wood only from fallen trees, and only so much a season, for everyone, traders included. The traders take less than they wanted. The forest stays. [Solidarity: raise]

Accept the Traders' Offer:

The village accepts. The tools are excellent. By the end of the season, a whole slope is bare, and the stream below it runs brown. (name1) calls the households together, too late for that slope, but not for the rest. [effect: small tech gain] [Solidarity: lower]

LCG-12. The Canoe That Belongs to Everyone
(picks two adults)

Six families built the great canoe together, and now it is finished, and all six want to take it out first. (name1) says the order should be drawn by lot. (name2) says the family whose own canoe was lost in the storm should go first, and every week after. What should the families decide?

Draw Lots:

They draw shells from a basket. The family that wins is the one that did the least work, and everyone knows it. They take the canoe out anyway, sheepishly, and bring back a huge catch, which they share with everyone. [effect: small food gain] [Solidarity: neutral]

By Need:

The family who lost their canoe goes first, and every week until theirs is replaced. The others agree, mostly. When the new canoe is finished, all six families paddle the great canoe out together, too many people in it, nearly sinking. [effect: small Building skill for (name1)] [Solidarity: raise]

LCG-13. The Children of Two Shores
(picks two children)

Let Them Play:

The new game spreads to every child on both islands. They also keep playing their own games, which turns out not to be a problem at all. By the next festival, there is a tournament, and the adults are playing too. [effect: everyone celebrates] [Solidarity: raise]

Keep Their Own Games:

The elders ask the children to play their own games at the festival. The children do, reluctantly. Then, behind the canoe house, out of sight, they play the new game until dark. The elders hear the shouting and pretend not to. [Solidarity: neutral]

LCG-14. The Seed Keepers' Disagreement
(picks two farmers)

Visiting farmers from another island have asked for some of the village's red taro, which grows nowhere else. (name1) says sharing seeds is how good plants survive. (name2) says the village has only a little, and if the visitors' crop fails, the red taro might be gone everywhere. What should the village do?

Share the Seed:

The village shares a handful. The visitors send back, a year later, a basket of their own striped beans. Then a blight hits Isola's red taro, and the visitors send back red taro from their own fields. (name2) admits sharing saved it. [effect: small food gain] [Solidarity: raise]

Keep It for the Village:

The village keeps its red taro. The visitors understand and leave. The red taro grows well. (name2) keeps a reserve of it buried in a jar, just in case. (name1) still thinks one day the village will wish it had shared. [Solidarity: neutral]

The whole village gathers at the river mouth and (name1) tells the trader: the river is not for sale. The trader looks at the crowd and leaves. That night there is singing by the river, and some regret about the tools. [Solidarity: raise]

The village hears the trader out. The offer is very generous. It is also forever. After a long night of talking, the village says no anyway, but now everyone understands exactly what they turned down, and why. [Solidarity: raise]

(name1) can read the forest floor like a story: who walked by, when, and in what hurry. (name2) and the other children want to learn. The best tracks are near the seabird nesting grounds, where the birds are easily frightened. Where should (name1) teach them?

At the Nesting Grounds, Quietly:

(name1) teaches the children to walk without a sound, to step only on rock, to look and not touch. They learn the tracks of rats, lizards and one very surprising pig. The birds barely notice. Somewhere Less Fragile:

(name1) takes them to the muddy stream bank instead, where the tracks are clear and nothing can be disturbed. The children learn quickly. One of them falls in the mud. All of them fall in the mud. [effect: small Research skill for (name2)] [Solidarity: raise]

(name2) calls the families who fish the cove. They agree the trader may fish there one day a moon, as a guest, not by right. The trader accepts, reluctantly. The cove's families feel, for the first time, that it is really theirs. [Solidarity: raise]

LCG-35. The Peace Delegation
(picks an elder and an adult)

The village and two neighboring settlements have been arguing over the same fishing grounds for a year. (name1) wants to send a delegation to each settlement, to listen first. (name2) wants one meeting with everyone at once, to settle it quickly. What should the village do?

Everyone meets on a neutral beach. The meeting is loud and long, and nearly ends in a fight. In the end, they agree to share the grounds by season. Both other settlements leave feeling rushed. It holds, for now. [Solidarity: neutral]

(name2) says the rule stays, reason or not. The children are cross. Then one evening they see a long grey shadow in the lagoon at dusk, and nobody argues about the rule again. [Solidarity: neutral]

LCG-50. The Game That Became a Tradition
(picks two children)

(name1) and (name2) have invented a game where everyone is tied together by vines, and the team only wins if nobody falls over. Nobody can win alone. The children love it. The adults are curious. What should the children do?

Teach It to Everyone:

The children teach the adults. The adults are terrible at it. The elders are worse. By the end of the festival, the whole village is tied together in one enormous, wobbling line, and nobody falls over, and the cheer can be heard across the bay. [effect: everyone celebrates] [Solidarity: raise]

Keep It for the Children:

The children keep it to themselves, and play it every evening on the beach. The adults watch from a distance, very curious. It becomes the children's game, passed down from child to child, and no adult ever quite learns the rules. [Solidarity: neutral]
