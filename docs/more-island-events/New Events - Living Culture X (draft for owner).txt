The rest of the owner's 100 Living Culture ideas and the world-inspired 50: secret languages and forgotten words, coming of age, elders who will not retire, healers' oaths, failed discoveries, masks and clothes and masterpieces nobody wanted, talent shows, ridiculous hats and the day nobody worked.

LCJ-01. The Forgotten Word
(picks an elder and a child)

(name1) said an old word at dinner: "mohalu". Nobody else has ever heard it. (name1) knows it is a real word: (his/her) grandmother used it. (He/she) just cannot remember what it means. (name2) is fascinated. What should they do with it?

Guess Together:

The village spends a week guessing. Some say it means a lazy afternoon. Some say a kind of fish. One elder says it was the name of a goat. (name1) is still not sure, but now everyone uses it, for everything. [Solidarity: raise]

Let the Children Use It:

(name2) and the children start using mohalu to mean the feeling of a warm rock after swimming. Within a month, the whole village uses it that way. (name1) says it might even be right. [effect: small Parenting skill for (name1)] [Solidarity: raise]

LCJ-02. The Children's Secret Language
(picks two children)

(name1), (name2) and the other children have invented a language: made-up words, finger signs and a whistle that means "an adult is coming". The adults are baffled. The researchers are fascinated. The parents are a little worried. What should happen?

Let Them Keep It:

The adults leave it alone. The children's language grows. Years later, when they are grown, they still whistle to each other across the village, and their own children start learning it. [Solidarity: raise]

Ask Them to Teach It:

The children agree to teach it, for a price: three days of no chores. The researchers learn it and write it down. The children are immediately bored of it, and invent a new one. [effect: small Research skill for (name1)] [Solidarity: neutral]

LCJ-03. The Story Without Words
(picks two adults)

(name1) does not speak with words, and never has. (He/she) communicates with signs, drawings and the expressions on (his/her) face. (He/she) has a story to tell the gathering: how (he/she) found the lost child in the swamp last week. (name2) has offered to speak it for (him/her). What should happen?

(name1) Tells It Their Way:

(name1) tells it with signs, drawings in the sand and a very dramatic pantomime of the swamp. The gathering is silent and riveted. When (he/she) acts out finding the child, the whole village cheers. Nobody needed a single word. [effect: (name1) celebrates] [Solidarity: raise]

(name2) Speaks for Them:

(name2) speaks the story while (name1) signs it. It works well, but (name2) gets a few details wrong, and (name1) corrects (him/her) by pointing, firmly. The gathering laughs. Next time, (name1) tells it alone. [Solidarity: neutral]

LCJ-04. The Welcome of a New Child
(picks an adult and an elder)

(name1) has taken in a small orphaned child from another family on the island. The relatives want to welcome the child properly, with blessings, stories and practical gifts. (name2) says a frightened child might be overwhelmed by everyone at once. How should they welcome the child?

All at Once:

The whole family arrives at once with gifts: a blanket, a carved fish, a song, a basket of fruit. The child hides behind (name1). Then a cousin offers a sweet fruit, and the child comes out, and is passed around and hugged until (he/she) falls asleep. [Solidarity: raise]

One at a Time:

Each relative comes on a different day, quietly, with one gift and one story. By the end of the month, the child knows everyone's name and the family's favorite songs. (name2) sings the last one, and the child sings along. [effect: small Parenting skill for (name1)] [Solidarity: raise]

Take Them Seriously:

(name2) brings the demands to the adults' gathering. The swimming spot is agreed at once. The chores are argued about. The knocking rule passes, to (name2)'s surprise. The children are very solemn about their victory. [Solidarity: raise]

(name2) smiles and says the adults will think about it. The children see through it immediately. The next assembly, they bring a carved list and demand an answer. (name2) brings it to the gathering after all. [Solidarity: neutral]

LCJ-13. The Forgotten Elders' Gathering
(picks two elders)

(name1) and (name2) and a few other old villagers have started meeting every evening by the old canoe house, because at the main fire nobody listens to them anymore. They tell stories, play the stone game and complain about the young. Some young people have noticed. What should the elders do?

Keep It to Themselves:

The elders' circle stays theirs. It is the liveliest place in the village after dark. The young people start sneaking close to listen to the stories. The elders pretend not to notice. [Solidarity: neutral]

Invite the Young:

The elders invite anyone who wants to come and listen. A few young people come, then more. The stories get longer and better. The complaining about the young continues, now with the young laughing along. [Solidarity: raise]

LCJ-14. The Wisdom of Being Wrong
(picks an elder and an adult)

For thirty years, (name1) has told the young farmers to plant taro at the full moon. (name2) has found, by careful counting, that it grows better at the new moon. (name1) has checked. (name2) is right. What should (name1) do?

(name1) stands up at the gathering and says, plainly, that (he/she) was wrong for thirty years, and (name2) is right. The village is silent. Then the young farmers cheer, for both of them. (name1) says it feels lighter than (he/she) expected. [Solidarity: raise]

Change the Advice Quietly:

(name1) starts telling the young farmers to plant at the new moon, without explaining. A few notice the change and ask why. (name1) tells them, privately, and gives (name2) the credit. [effect: small Farming skill for (name2)] [Solidarity: neutral]

(picks two cooks)

The big harvest project is finished, and every household is bringing its favorite dish to the festival of shared labor. (name1) and (name2) have already argued about whose fish stew is better, and so have six other families. Someone has suggested judging. What should happen?

The judging takes all evening and nearly causes three feuds. The winner is a dish nobody expected: a grandmother's plain boiled taro. The losers grumble and eat seconds of it. [effect: everyone celebrates; small food loss] [Solidarity: neutral]

Just Eat:

Nobody judges. Everyone eats everything. (name1) and (name2) try each other's stew and each admit, after a lot of chewing, that the other one is good. Not better. Good. [effect: everyone celebrates; small food loss] [Solidarity: raise]

LCJ-16. The Shared Harvest
(picks two farmers)

The harvest is enormous. (name1) wants every family to get an equal share. (name2) points out that some families have eight children and some have none, and that the old widow on the hill has no one to farm for her. What should the village do?

Share Equally:

Every family gets the same. The large families are a little hungry by the end of the season; the small ones have food to spare, and most of them share it without being asked. It works out, mostly. [effect: small food gain] [Solidarity: neutral]

Share by Need:

Larger families and those who cannot farm get more. Some small families grumble. Nobody goes hungry. The old widow on the hill sends down a basket of her best herbs in thanks. [effect: small food gain] [Solidarity: raise]

(picks a parent and a child)

It has been a hungry season, and (name1)'s family has one ripe breadfruit left. (name2) is hungry. The old neighbor next door is hungrier. And the seeds could grow a tree, one day. What should they do?

The family eats the breadfruit together, slowly. (name2) says it is the best thing (he/she) has ever tasted. (name1) watches the neighbor's hut and feels guilty, and also fed. [Solidarity: neutral]

Plant the Seeds:

(name1) and (name2) eat the flesh and plant the seeds behind the hut. Most do not sprout. One does. Years later, (name2), grown, picks the first breadfruit from that tree and brings it to (name1). [effect: small Farming skill for (name2)] [Solidarity: neutral]

Share It With the Neighbor:

They split it three ways with the old neighbor. Nobody is full. The neighbor cries. When the season turns, the neighbor's grown children bring (name1)'s family a whole basket of breadfruit, and say they heard. [effect: small food gain] [Solidarity: raise]

The gardens are planted again. The harvest is small and getting smaller. (name1) quietly rests one corner on (his/her) own. Next year, everyone can see the difference. [effect: small food gain] [Solidarity: neutral]

LCJ-22. The Healer's Oath
(picks two young healers)

(name1) and (name2), both new healers, have been talking about what kind of healers they want to be: honest about what they do not know, keeping their patients' secrets, never pretending a cure is certain. They want to make a promise. How?

They carve a short promise on a board in the healing hut: tell the truth, keep secrets, know your limits. Every new healer reads it. The old healers grumble, then quietly read it too. [effect: small Healing skill for (name1)] [Solidarity: raise]

Each Their Own:

Each healer speaks their own promise, aloud, at a small gathering. They are all different. (name2)'s ends with "and never laugh at a strange rash", which is cheered. [effect: small Healing skill for (name2)] [Solidarity: raise]

(name1) explains again and again. (name2) gets angry. In the end (name2) agrees to a gentler poultice instead. It works, more slowly. (name1) learns that there is often a third way. [effect: small Healing skill for (name1)] [Solidarity: neutral]

LCJ-26. The Experiment Everyone Saw
(picks a researcher and an adult)

(name1) invited the whole village to see (his/her) discovery: a mixture of ash and seaweed that makes fire burn hotter. In front of everyone, (he/she) threw it on the fire, which made an enormous cloud of green smoke and went out completely. (name2) is trying very hard not to laugh. What should (name1) do?

Laugh It Off:

(name1) laughs, coughing green smoke. The village laughs with (him/her). Afterwards, (he/she) works out why it happened: the seaweed was wet. The dry version works. Nobody comes to that demonstration, but it works. [effect: small Research skill for (name1)] [Solidarity: raise]

Try Again:

(name1) asks for another fire and tries again, with dry seaweed. It burns blue-hot and roars. The village gasps, then cheers. (name2) says the green smoke was better. [effect: small Research skill for (name1); small tech gain] [Solidarity: raise]

LCJ-38. The Price of a Promise

(name2) sends word to every island the village trades with. The trader finds no buyers anywhere. Two months later, the tools arrive, along with an apology. [effect: small tech gain] [Solidarity: raise]

Nobody judges. Everyone just wears ridiculous hats for the rest of the night. In the morning, the beach is covered in abandoned hats, and the children wear them for a week. [effect: everyone celebrates] [Solidarity: raise]

Once a year, the silver fish swim up the stream to lay their eggs, so many the water seems to boil. (name1) says take as many as the baskets will hold. (name2) has noticed the run is smaller each year, and wants to count them and take only a share. What should the village do?

Storytellers from four villages have come to Isola to tell their stories, each in their own language. (name2) cannot understand half of them, and is frustrated. (name1) says some stories are best heard in their own words, even if you do not understand them all. What should happen?
