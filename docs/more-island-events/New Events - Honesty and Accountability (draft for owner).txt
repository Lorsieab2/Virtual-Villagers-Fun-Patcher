Small lies, kind fibs, things left unsaid, things forgotten, honest mistakes and the odd careless one. Consequences stay the size of the mistake. Privacy is not lying, truth doesn't automatically fix things, and nobody has to confess, apologize or forgive.
Written in the style of the owner's own events. Edit anything and send it back; nothing ships until you approve.

NOTES FOR THE OWNER
- Effects are kept small and marked in [brackets]: [small food loss] (HON-01, 12, 15, 22, 27, 39) and [small health loss] (HON-13 for (name1), HON-20 for (name2)). The health losses are a stomach upset from underdone fish and a scraped knee; both are temporary in the story. Everything else is story only. If six food losses feels like too many, HON-15 and HON-39 are the easiest to make story only.
- No single popups in this set; every event has two choices.
- Privacy is not lying: HON-05 (where were you?) and HON-25 (an overheard practice speech) show keeping something to yourself as perfectly fine.
- No forced confession, apology or forgiveness: many choices leave the truth unspoken (HON-06, 10, 18, 20, 27, 30, 48), and lies are found out now, later or never. Where the truth is told, it doesn't always make things right (HON-09, 11, 33, 37, 41).
- Honest mistakes and carelessness are kept apart: HON-07, 14, 18, 23, 44 are honest mistakes; HON-12, 20, 22, 27, 34, 48 involve putting something off or cutting a corner; consequences stay proportionate either way.
- HON-15 (falling asleep on watch) was written so that tiredness is never treated as negligence: the other villager sees how tired (name1) is and sends (him/her) to bed.
- HON-45 (always a little late) ends with the others adapting, or with nothing changing, and both are fine. Nobody is shamed for being slow in the mornings.
- HON-31 (one more cast) involves choppy water but nobody is hurt; flag it for the deep-water filter if you'd like.
- HON-36 is the only event with two children; they get brooms and a piece of fruit, nothing more.
- HON-49 (two elders confessing an old prank) is a story the villagers tell about themselves; it doesn't need any history the game tracks.
- Avoided premises already in the catalog or drafts: the child's "a bird did it" lie (IL-37), the cracked communal water jar (RM-02), the broken keepsake, the broken toy, the double-counted harvest (RM-34), the forgotten promise to a child, the forgotten invitation, the feast cooked for the wrong date, the healer's mixed-up medicine, the carver's mistake, the careless worker and the shared tool, and the surprise that isn't.
