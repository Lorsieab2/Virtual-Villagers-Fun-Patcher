NEW EVENTS — PONDERING HOW LIFE HAS GONE (LIF-01..50, draft for the owner, 2026-11-15)
Adults of every age looking at their own lives so far: roads not taken, quiet satisfaction, small regrets, unexpected pride, things left unfinished, luck, gratitude and plenty of amusement. Nobody has to reach a verdict.
Written in the style of the owner's own events. Edit anything and send it back; nothing ships until you approve.

LIF-49. The Version Who Left
(picks an adult aged 35 or older)

Long ago, (name1) had the chance to leave Isola, to go out across the sea with a group of explorers who never came back to stay. (He/she) said no. Today, standing on the beach looking out at the horizon, (he/she) finds (him/her)self imagining the other version of (him/her)self: the one who went. Somewhere out there, living some other life.

Wave to the Other One:
(name1) imagines that other self, older now too, standing on some faraway beach, looking back this way and wondering about the one who stayed. (He/she) feels silly. (He/she) waves anyway, at the empty sea. "Hope it's going well," (he/she) says. "It's going fine here." Then (he/she) goes home for dinner, which is fish, which is good.

Wonder If They're Happy:
(name1) wonders if the other version is happy. Happier? Less happy? Just different? (He/she) can't know. Nobody ever can. (He/she) thinks about all the things (he/she) would have missed if (he/she) had gone. Then about all the things (he/she) missed by staying. It comes out about even. That's probably the truth of it. (He/she) picks up a shell and walks home.

LIF-50. So Far, So Good
(picks an adult aged 40 or older; a single popup, no choice)

(name1) is lying in (his/her) hammock in the late afternoon, swinging gently, looking up through the palm leaves at bits of blue sky, and taking a sort of inventory. Some things went the way (he/she) planned. Most didn't. A few went much better. There are a couple of mistakes (he/she) still winces about, a few people (he/she) misses, a few dreams that drifted off somewhere, and a startling number of very good meals. (He/she) has been lucky and unlucky in roughly equal amounts, which seems fair. (He/she) is not famous, not rich, not the best at anything, and not finished yet. A breeze comes through. A bird lands on the hammock rope, looks at (him/her), and leaves again. (name1) laughs out loud at nothing in particular and decides that, so far, all things considered, it's been pretty good.
