NEW EVENTS — JUSTICE AND CONSEQUENCES (JUS-01 to JUS-50, draft for the owner, 2026-11-15)
Everyday rules, disputes between neighbors, the elders and the council, making things right, losing a role, a night to cool down, wrongful suspicion and evidence that doesn't add up. Consequences are proportionate and never cruel; children get guidance; nobody goes without food, water or shelter; an accusation is not guilt.
Written in the style of the owner's own events. Edit anything and send it back; nothing ships until you approve.

NOTES FOR THE OWNER
- Effects are kept small and marked in [brackets]: [small skill gain] for (name1) (JUS-05, first choice) and [small food loss] (JUS-40, first choice). Everything else is story only. For JUS-05, the skill would be whichever gardening/farming skill the game has.
- No single popups in this set; every event has two choices.
- "Chief" never appears; the events use "the elders" and "the council," so nothing here needs a VV3-only version.
- Confinement: JUS-08 (a night alone in the storehouse to cool down after a frightening shouting match; the villager agrees, and has a blanket, water, food, a lamp and an elder within call) and JUS-41 (a hungry visitor asked to stay the night at the meeting place, fed and with a blanket, until morning). Both are one night only, with dignity and every necessity. No new building is used or implied.
- Banishment: only JUS-30, and only as an elder's uncertain memory. It follows 5J: it was the only time anyone remembers, there was continuing trouble and frightened people, lesser measures were tried first, the person was given a canoe, food and water, the village disagreed and still does, and nobody knows what became of them. Nothing is shown. The empty "flat stones where a hut once stood" are story only.
- No one ever goes without food, water or shelter: JUS-38 makes this explicit (an elder refuses to take away someone's share of the catch), and JUS-19 sets a smaller repayment so a household can still eat.
- No forced apology or confession: JUS-11 (the council won't order a public apology), JUS-33 (a correction of the facts, not a public apology), JUS-37 (a villager may keep where they were private), JUS-46 (rudeness isn't punished, but the hurt villager may keep their distance).
- Wrongful suspicion and shaky evidence: JUS-07, 10, 18, 21, 24, 37; in each, the wrongly suspected villager is never blamed for the suspicion, and the council admits its mistake in JUS-18.
- Boundaries versus ostracism: JUS-15 and JUS-28 (a villager may stop fishing with, or lending to, someone; the council won't order otherwise, but asks that the boundary not be spread to everyone else).
- Children get guidance, never punishment: JUS-04, 14, 26, 36, 44. Teens: JUS-23 and 48.
- JUS-42 and JUS-35: pigs are "tied up" rather than penned, to avoid implying new pens. JUS-45 uses a dog; where a game has no dogs, a pig works just as well.
- JUS-41's visitor is an Isolan "from another village on the far side of the island"; no outside place is named.
- Avoided premises already in the catalog or drafts: the stolen woven doll, the broken swing, the punishment everyone regrets, the disputed verdict over shared tools, the rule nobody remembers, the rule that hurt, the missing pearl, the stolen spoon, the slug race, the missing trade goods, the youngest witness, the missing coconuts, the cooking pot that goes missing, the cheating accusation at a contest, the hammock that belonged to everyone, and the rulebook's own examples (the disputed judgment over supplies, the prisoner's request, the unwelcome neighbor, the old law).
