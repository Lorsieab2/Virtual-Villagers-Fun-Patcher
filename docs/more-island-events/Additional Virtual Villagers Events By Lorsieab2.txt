RULES FOR EVERY EVENT
- Your story text is unaltered, except the obvious mistakes you asked me to fix (each event's heading says which).
- (name) is always the villager's full name.
- Protected villagers are never picked unless you said otherwise: nursing villagers (mother or father), the Golden Child, The Secret City's Tribal Chief, The Lost Children's totems, and anyone that game's own island events never pick. - What an ending does (deaths, disappearances, rewards, skills) happens when its page shows, and the Island Events log gets one entry per event that always matches the save.
- A villager who "masters" a skill has it set to 100.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
REQUIREMENTS TO HAPPEN
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The event can happen only when at least TWO ELIGIBLE villagers are in the village: two who could each be picked by the rules in "Who is never picked" below. With fewer than two, it cannot happen.

When it can happen, it takes an equal share of the island-event draw, like the game's own events. It uses the game's usual timing between island events. Pick Island Event can also choose it, under the same requirement.

Exception: Custom Island Events (Story / Cheat Upgrades). There the player decides, so this requirement does not apply.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS NEVER PICKED (the adventurer, or the friend in ending A8)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

In every game:
- anyone under 14 (children and babies);
- anyone nursing a baby, mother or father;
- anyone dead (a body or grave), or not in the village.

And in each game:
- A New Home: the Golden Child.
- The Lost Children: the Esteemed Elders' totem statues.
- The Secret City: the tribal chief.
- The Tree of Life: no one else.
- New Believers: every Heathen (believers only), the chief, the Retired Chief and the other special roles the game itself never sends on events, and Reanimated spirits.

The friend in ending A8 is never the adventurer. Because the event needs two villagers who can be picked, there is always a friend, so A8 can always happen. (Only a Custom Island Event, which skips that requirement, could leave no one to be the friend; then A8 becomes A5.)
With fewer than two villagers who can be picked, the event does not happen (except through Custom Island Events).

THE REMEMBRANCE CEREMONY (all five games)
==========================================================================
Your text, unchanged.

REQUIREMENTS TO HAPPEN
- At least one grave (someone buried), in all five games.
- New Believers also: every Heathen has converted (the hovel is empty for good).
- It does NOT need the "Memorial Actions Can Also Occur at the Heathen Hovel" patch (see the effects).
- It can happen again, as likely as one stock event each time.

WHO TAKES PART
- Everyone joins in: children, nursing mothers and fathers, and protected villagers too; no one is left out (the owner's exception). Each villager is sent on their own, so the game's usual group filters (such as The Secret City leaving out 14 to 17 year olds) don't apply. In New Believers, believers only: Heathens never take part.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
One popup (no choice, just OK):

Today is the Day of Remembrance. The villagers are gathering to honor those that came before them, and to thank them for all the sacrifices they have made. Throughout the day they share stories about their ancestors and loved ones, partake in a grand feast, and acknowledge that all experiences, good and bad, form parts of their future.

EFFECTS (not mentioned in the popup)
- Each villager taking part Honors the Dead or Reflects (50% each). New Believers: Honoring the Dead at the mausoleum; with the "Memorial Actions Can Also Occur at the Heathen Hovel" patch, some go to the former Heathen hovel to Honor the Dead or Reflect, as that patch does. The Tree of Life: Honoring the Dead at the graveyard. The other games: their own graveyard or memorial actions (Honoring the Dead, and Reflecting where the game has it).
- The feast quietly uses 10 food for every villager, never below 0 (the same as Blessings Day in A New Home, The Lost Children and The Secret City).

THE RUMBLINGS (all five games)
==========================================================================
Your text, unchanged.

REQUIREMENTS TO HAPPEN
- Every puzzle in that game is completed (the owner: "to compensate for this, this event needs all puzzles in their games to be completed").
- At least one finished population hut, clothing hut, love hut, hospital or nursery school (whichever that game has).
- It can happen again, as likely as one stock event each time.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
One popup (no choice, just OK):

One morning, the village becomes wracked with a violent shaking. Things start flying off shelves, pottery smashes on the floor, and everyone starts taking cover under nearby structures. The villagers are frightened, but luckily, no one is hurt. A lot of loose rubble is strewn about and some structures have been visibly damaged. It looks like the builders have more work cut out for them.

EFFECTS
- Each finished population hut, clothing hut, love hut, hospital and nursery school is damaged at 25% each; at least one always is, and an unlucky village can have all of them damaged.
- A damaged structure loses 10 to 100% of its building progress (at random), and the builders must rebuild it.
- At 100% the structure is back to unbuilt, as in the original game: a full rebuild. In The Tree of Life and New Believers, where unbuilt huts can be moved, this lets the player rearrange the village without starting over.
- Buildings that are puzzles are never damaged (A New Home's first hut; The Lost Children's Hospital, Sewing Hut and Lovely Hut), and New Believers' nursery is left out. Solved puzzles stay solved. No one is hurt or dies.
- The Secret City's two starting huts lose only the work done after the new-village start.
- A badly damaged building may need you to drop a villager on it to restart, as in the original game. Rebuilding repeats its celebration and tip; the "3 huts" trophy still needs three different huts.
- If a damaged population hut leaves the village over its limit, nobody leaves; no new babies come until it is rebuilt.
