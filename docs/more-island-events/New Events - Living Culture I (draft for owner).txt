NEW EVENTS - LIVING CULTURE I: SONGS, CHANTS, SPEECHES AND WELCOMES (LCA-01..50, draft, 2026-11-18)
Isola sings, chants and speaks for itself: welcoming a new leader, wishing travelers luck, greeting newcomers and newborns, rallying for hard work, standing up for each other, and arguing about who gets the next verse.
Written in the style of the owner's own events. The owner waived the review ("I'm sure the draft events are fine"); every event passed the Isola rules and the final 15-point review, logged in the notes.

LCA-01. The Song of the New Leader
(picks an elder and an adult)

The village has chosen a new chief, and tonight everyone will gather to welcome (him/her) with a song. The trouble is deciding which one. (name1) has sung the old welcome song for every chief since (he/she) was a child and does not see why anything should change. (name2) thinks a new chief deserves new words, after the year the village has had. How should the village welcome its new chief?

Sing the Old Song:

The village sings the old song, slow and deep, the way it has always been sung. Halfway through, (name1)'s voice wobbles and (he/she) has to stop and wipe (his/her) eyes, remembering every chief (he/she) has sung it for. The new chief listens with (his/her) hand on (his/her) heart. Even (name2) admits it was the right song for tonight. [effect: everyone celebrates] [Solidarity: raise]

Write New Words:

(name2) stays up two nights writing new words about the storms, the lean months and the people who got everyone through them. Some of the elders sing along. Some of them stand with their arms folded and sing the old words under their breath instead. The two songs clash horribly in the middle. The new chief laughs and says (he/she) will take both. [Solidarity: neutral]

Every Family Gets a Verse:

Every household adds a verse. One is about fish. Three are about fish. One is mostly about a goat. By the time the last family finishes, the moon has moved halfway across the sky and the children are asleep in a heap. The new chief stays awake for every single verse, and thanks each family by name. [effect: everyone celebrates] [Solidarity: raise]

LCA-02. The Song of Welcome
(picks an adult and a child)

A family has come to Isola from somewhere across the water, tired and very quiet, with a canoe full of everything they own. (name1) wants the village to welcome them properly. (name2) has already decided the new family's little ones are (his/her) friends and keeps waving at them from behind a tree. What kind of welcome should they give?

Teach Them Our Welcome Song:

The whole village gathers on the beach and sings the welcome song. The new family stands very still, unsure what to do with their hands. Then (name2) runs over and starts teaching their children the actions, one wave at a time. By the second chorus the newcomers are clapping along, a beat behind and grinning. [Solidarity: raise]

Ask for One of Their Songs:

(name1) asks the family if they have a song of their own. After a long pause, the oldest of them begins to sing, softly, in words nobody on Isola knows. The village listens in silence. When it ends, (name2) asks if (he/she) can learn it. The family looks at each other, and for the first time since they landed, they smile. [effect: small Parenting skill for (name1)] [Solidarity: raise]

LCA-03. The Returning Canoes
(picks an adult and an elder)

The fishing canoes should have been back by sunset. Now it is dark, the wind is up, and the families are gathering on the beach in twos and threes, not saying much. (name2) says that in the old days the village sang the canoes home, so the crews could follow the sound. (name1) says a fire would be easier to see. What should they do?

Sing Them Home:

The village sings into the dark, one song after another, until their voices crack. Just before midnight, someone hears a shout from the water. The first canoe scrapes onto the sand, then the second, then the third. The crews say they heard the singing from beyond the reef and simply rowed toward it. (name2) does not say I told you so. (He/she) does not need to. [effect: everyone celebrates] [Solidarity: raise]

Light Fires Along the Beach:

(name1) and the others build fires all along the shore. The canoes come in one by one, steering by the light. The last one comes in very late, its crew soaked and shaking, but whole. (name2) wraps a blanket around the youngest of them and hums the old song anyway, just quietly, just for (him/her). [Solidarity: raise]

LCA-06. The Festival of Small Victories
(picks two adults and a child)

Festivals on Isola are usually for big things: a great harvest, a new hut, a canoe that survived a storm. (name1) wants one for small things instead: the first time someone swam out to the rocks, a pot that did not crack, a week with no arguments. (name2) thinks this is either lovely or the start of a very long night. (name3) already has a list. How should they run it?

Everyone Names Their Own Victory:

One by one, the villagers stand up and say what they are proud of. (name3) is proud of losing a tooth. (name2) is proud of not losing (his/her) temper with the goats. Someone is proud of finally learning to whistle, and does, for some time. It is a very long night, and nobody minds. [effect: everyone celebrates] [Solidarity: raise]

The Village Chooses:

The village votes on whose small victories to celebrate. It is supposed to be fun, but some people get chosen three times and some not at all, and (name1) notices a few quiet faces at the edge of the firelight. Next year, (he/she) says, everyone names their own. [Solidarity: lower]

LCA-09. The Song That Everyone Knows

(name1) and (name2) fell out over a fence a month ago, and have not said a word to each other since. (name1) starts again, a little louder. (name2) joins in. They get through the whole song, standing in the stream with their pots, and at the end neither of them says anything about the fence. They walk back to the village together. It is a start. [Solidarity: raise]

(name2) picks up (his/her) pot and leaves without a word. (name1) stands there a while, listening to the water. The fence is still between them. But the next evening, from across the village, (name1) hears someone humming the harmony, very quietly. [Solidarity: neutral]

LCA-10. The Feast of Many Homes
(picks two adults)

Over the years, families have come to Isola from many shores, and each has kept a few songs from home. (name1) wants a feast where every family sings one. (name2) thinks it would be even better to weave them all into one great song. (name1) worries some families do not want their songs changed. How should the feast go?

Each Family Sings Its Own:

One after another, the families stand and sing. Some songs are fast, some slow, one is almost entirely clapping. The children try to copy every one and get most of them gloriously wrong. Nobody's song is changed, and everybody's song is heard. [effect: everyone celebrates] [Solidarity: raise]

Weave Them Into One:

(name2) asks each family for one line. Most say yes. Two families say no, and (name2) respects that. The finished song is strange and lovely, and the families who stayed out sing their own songs afterwards, to loud applause. Nobody is quite sure which part was best. [Solidarity: raise]

LCA-12. The Builders' Rhythm
(picks two adults)

The builders are raising the roof beam of the new hut, and everyone heaves on the beat of the old lifting chant: hey, HAH, hey, HAH. (name1) has been leading it for years. (name2) says the HAH comes too early, and everyone is pulling against each other. (name1) says (name2) is pulling wrong. The beam is still on the ground. What should they do?

Keep the Old Chant:

They try the old chant again, all pulling harder. The beam rises, slips, and drops back down with a thump that shakes the ground. Nobody is hurt, but (name1) sits on it for a while, thinking. The next day (he/she) moves the HAH a little later, without saying anything. The beam goes up first try. [effect: small Building skill for (name1)] [Solidarity: neutral]

Try (name2)'s Rhythm:

(name2) leads a slower chant, with a long pause before the pull. It feels wrong for about three heaves. Then the beam swings up as if it weighs nothing. (name1) stares at it, then laughs and claps (name2) on the back. "Fine," (he/she) says. "But I am keeping the HAH." [effect: small Building skill for (name2)] [Solidarity: raise]

[effect: small food loss] [Solidarity: neutral]

LCA-17. The Harvesting Chorus
(picks two adults)

Harvest day, and the farmers sing as they work so the picking keeps time. This year (name1) has made up a new harvest song, and it is a disaster: it is so catchy that every time it reaches the chorus, the whole field stops picking and dances. (name2) points out that the harvest is half the size it was by this time last year. What should they do?

Keep the New Song:

They keep it. The harvest takes two extra days, and the farmers are covered in dust and laughter by the end of it. A few berries rot on the bushes before they get to them. Nobody regrets a thing. [effect: small food loss; everyone celebrates] [Solidarity: raise]

Back to the Old Song:

(name2) leads the old song, slow and steady. The picking goes quickly. But every so often, from somewhere in the field, someone hums the chorus of (name1)'s song, and three people stop to dance before they can help themselves. [effect: small food gain] [Solidarity: neutral]
