1. Mister Pebbles
(picks a child aged 3-9)

(name1) has a new best friend. (his/her) name is Mister Pebbles, he is very tall, he only eats the blue part of the sky, and nobody but (name1) can see him. At dinner, (name1) sets out an extra leaf plate. "Mister Pebbles says the fish is too salty," (he/she) announces.

Play Along:
"Well, tell Mister Pebbles the cook did (his/her) best," says (name2), passing the plate. (name1) giggles and whispers something to the empty seat. For the rest of the evening the whole family is very careful not to sit on Mister Pebbles. (name1) is delighted. [effect: (name1) celebrates]

Ask About Mister Pebbles:
(name2) asks what Mister Pebbles likes to do. (name1) talks for a very long time. Mister Pebbles, it turns out, can swim faster than a dolphin, knows every star's name, and is afraid of crabs. "Just like you," (name2) says. (name1) thinks about this. "Mister Pebbles is braver than me," (he/she) says. "But I'm getting there."

Let It Be:
Nobody makes a fuss. (name1) and Mister Pebbles spend the afternoon building a very important fort out of palm fronds. By sunset Mister Pebbles has gone home, wherever that is. He'll probably be back tomorrow.

2. The Villager Who Talks to the Fish
(picks an adult)

(name1) has been fishing alone all morning, and anyone walking by can hear (him/her) chatting away. "Come on, little one. I know you're down there. I have a lovely worm for you. No? Fine. Be like that." (name2) stops on the path and listens for a while.

Join the Conversation:
"Have they answered yet?" (name2) asks. (name1) doesn't even turn around. "They're shy today." (name2) sits down and starts talking to the fish too. Neither of them catches anything, but it's the best morning either has had all week.

Leave (him/her) to It:
(name2) smiles and walks on. Behind (him/her), (name1) is now explaining the whole history of the village to a very patient crab. Later that day (name1) comes home with a basket full of fish and refuses to say which of them was listening. [effect: small food gain]

3. The Quiet Hands
(picks a villager who has not spoken for a long time; a young adult or older. In the story (name1) is mute; nothing about the villager changes in the game)

(name1) doesn't speak. (he/she) hasn't for years, and (he/she) is perfectly happy that way, thank you. Most of the village has learned (his/her) hand signs: "food," "come here," "that's very funny," and a rather rude one for when the builders are too loud. Today a new apprentice, (name2), keeps talking to (name1) slowly and loudly, as though (he/she) can't hear.

Teach (name2) the Signs:
(name1) sits (name2) down and starts with the basics. "Hello." "Thank you." "I can hear you perfectly well." (name2) turns very red, then very curious. By evening the two of them are having a whole conversation, mostly about the builders being too loud.

Let (name1) Handle It (his/her) Own Way:
(name1) picks up a stick and draws in the sand: I HEAR YOU. YOU CAN TALK NORMALLY. Then, underneath, a little drawing of (name2) with a very big mouth. (name2) laughs so hard (he/she) has to sit down. "Sorry," (he/she) says, at a perfectly normal volume. (name1) signs: "You're forgiven."

4. What (name1) Didn't Say
(picks an adult)

The village is planning the harvest feast, and everyone has an opinion. (name1) has an opinion too. (he/she) thinks the whole thing should be moved to the beach, with music, and torches, and those little honey cakes nobody has made in years. (he/she) opens (his/her) mouth. Then (he/she) closes it again.

Say It Anyway:
"What if we did it on the beach?" (name1) blurts out. There's a pause. Then (name2) says, "...with torches?" And suddenly everyone is talking at once. Nobody remembers whose idea it was, but (name1) does. (he/she) smiles quietly into (his/her) cup all evening.

Keep It to (him/her)self:
The feast is held in the usual place, the usual way, and it's lovely. Later, walking home along the shore, (name1) pictures the torches and the music and the honey cakes. Maybe next year. Maybe (he/she) will just make the honey cakes (him/her)self.

5. Somewhere Else Entirely
(picks a villager who is working)

(name1) is supposed to be sorting seeds. (he/she) is, technically. (his/her) hands are sorting seeds. The rest of (him/her) is sailing a great canoe across a sea nobody has ever seen, past islands shaped like sleeping turtles, toward a shore where the sand is every color at once.

Snap Back to Work:
"(name1)! Those go in the OTHER basket!" (name1) blinks. The great canoe sinks without a trace. (he/she) has sorted the whole morning's seeds into the wrong basket. (name2) sighs, then laughs, and helps (him/her) sort them back.

Keep Dreaming a Little Longer:
The canoe reaches the colorful shore. (name1) steps off. Then the seeds are finished, somehow, and it's lunchtime, and nobody noticed anything at all. (name1) decides that some days, the best journeys are the ones you take sitting down.

6. The Talking-To
(picks an adult or teen)

(name1) is pacing behind the huts, muttering. "Okay. Okay. You're going to walk up and say: 'I'm sorry I broke your bowl.' Not 'it was already cracked.' Just 'sorry.' You can do this." (he/she) takes a deep breath. Then (he/she) practices it again. And again. (name2), the bowl's owner, is standing right behind (him/her).

Turn Around:
(name1) turns around, sees (name2), and makes a noise like a startled bird. "...I'm sorry I broke your bowl," (he/she) manages. (name2) raises an eyebrow. "It WAS already cracked," (he/she) admits. They both burst out laughing.

(name2) Quietly Walks Away:
(name2) slips off before (name1) notices. An hour later (name1) finally knocks, says "I'm sorry I broke your bowl," exactly as practiced, and (name2) says "Thank you," with a perfectly straight face. (he/she) never mentions overhearing the rehearsal. Not even once. Not even a little.

7. The Big Maybe
(picks a teen or young adult)

(name1) wants to be a healer. (he/she) has wanted it for as long as (he/she) can remember. But healers see sickness, and sadness, and sometimes things go wrong no matter what anyone does. What if (he/she) isn't strong enough? What if (he/she) freezes? Tonight (name1) can't sleep, and finds (name2), an old healer, sitting by the fire.

Ask (name2) If (he/she) Was Ever Afraid:
"Every single day for the first year," (name2) says. "Some days still." (name1) stares. "But you're the best healer we have!" (name2) shrugs. "Being afraid and doing it anyway are two different things. So is deciding not to. Both are fine." (name1) doesn't decide anything that night. But (he/she) sleeps a little better. [effect: small Healing skill, only where the game has it]

Just Sit by the Fire:
Neither of them says much. The fire crackles. Somewhere a baby is fussing. After a while (name2) says, "You don't have to know yet." (name1) nods. (he/she) doesn't know yet. That's alright.

8. The Worry Stone
(picks any villager aged 8 or older)

(name1) has been worried about something for days. (he/she) won't say what, and that's (his/her) business. But (he/she) keeps turning a smooth little stone over and over in (his/her) fingers. Today (name2) notices.

Offer to Listen:
"You don't have to tell me anything," says (name2). "But if you ever want to, I'm here." (name1) nods, and keeps turning the stone. (he/she) doesn't say anything that day. But the next morning there's a little flower on (name2)'s doorstep, and (name1) is humming.

Find (him/her) a Matching Stone:
(name2) spends the afternoon on the beach and comes back with a stone that's almost exactly the same. "For when one isn't enough," (he/she) says. (name1) laughs for the first time in days. The worry is still there. It just feels a little smaller with two stones.

9. Night Thoughts
(picks an adult; a quiet one-popup moment, no choice)

It's late. Everyone in (name1)'s hut is asleep except (name1). (he/she) is thinking about everything at once: whether the roof will hold if it rains, whether (he/she) said the wrong thing at dinner, whether the stars are the same stars that (his/her) grandparents looked at, whether anyone else ever lies awake like this. Somewhere outside, a frog starts singing. Then another. Then about forty more. (name1) gives up on the big questions, laughs quietly into the dark, and listens to the frogs until morning.

10. The Little Voice
(picks a child aged 5-12)

(name1) is building the tallest tower of shells anyone has ever built. Partway up, a little voice in (his/her) head says: It's going to fall. You're not good at this. Everyone will laugh. (name1) stops, hand in the air, holding one very small shell.

Put the Shell on Anyway:
(name1) puts the shell on top. The tower wobbles. It wobbles some more. It stays up! (name1) shrieks so loudly that three villagers come running to see. The little voice is very quiet for the rest of the day.

Take the Tower Down and Build a Wall Instead:
"Walls are better anyway," (name1) decides. (he/she) builds a long, wonderful wall that winds all the way around a tide pool. The crabs inside seem very impressed. The little voice has nothing to say about walls.
