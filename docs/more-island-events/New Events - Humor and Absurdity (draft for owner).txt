HUM-31. The Coconut Nobody Could Open
(picks an adult)

(name1) has been trying to open one particular coconut for most of the morning. (He/she) has hit it with a rock, dropped it from a height and wedged it in the fork of a tree. It hasn't even cracked. Word has spread. A line of villagers has formed, each of them certain they know the trick.

HUM-34. The Guava in the Sleeve

HUM-36. Pins and Needles
(picks two adults)

HUM-43. The Tug-of-War Nobody Won
(picks two adults as team leaders)

It's the festival tug-of-war: (name1)'s team at one end of the rope, (name2)'s team at the other, and a patch of mud in the middle. Both teams heave with everything they've got. Nobody moves. Then, with a soft twang, the rope snaps exactly in the middle.

Call It a Draw:

Demand a Rematch:
The rope is knotted, and they go again. The knot slips, and both teams fall over again. The mud patch grows. By the third try, everyone is so muddy that it is impossible to tell which team is which. The judge gives up, and both teams run into the sea together to wash.

NOTES FOR THE OWNER
- Effects are kept small and marked in [brackets]: only HUM-49 has one ([small food gain]). Everything else is story only.
- No single popups in this set; every event has two choices.
- Embarrassment, never humiliation: in every event the villager laughs with everyone else, keeps their dignity, or the joke turns out to be on nobody. Nobody is mocked for who they are, their body, age or ability. HUM-36 (a leg gone to sleep) and HUM-32 (an elder in the splash contest) were written with that in mind; worth a look if you want to be sure.
- Bodily humor is mild and implied only: HUM-03 (stomach growl), HUM-05 (a burp and its echo), HUM-13 (two villagers needing the latrine at once), HUM-14 (suspicious noises after bean stew), HUM-17 (a dye mark on someone's backside), HUM-39 (a log that makes a rude noise). Nothing graphic or sexual. HUM-13 is the closest to toilet humor; drop it if it's more than you want.
- Pranks are always with permission or harmless: HUM-10 is a prank the villager asked permission for; HUM-20 is a friendly echo trick.
- Funny magic stays ambiguous: HUM-22 is a scientist's tonic (story only, wears off by evening; the villager tests it on themselves, and others are free to refuse); HUM-23 is an elder's charm, and nobody knows whether the charm or the sap drew the butterflies. Neither event needs a magician, so both are all five games.
- HUM-19's blessing slip is the speaker's own, laughed at by the speaker first; the blessing itself is never mocked.
- HUM-29: retitled "Headdress" rather than "Hat" to suit the setting.
- HUM-32: the "deep, safe swimming pool" is story only.
- Animals are only ever silly, never harmed: the pig (HUM-02, 11, 14, 47, 50), frogs (41), a bird (38), a fish that slaps back and swims away (37).
- Avoided premises already in the catalog or drafts: the ill-timed sneeze, the three sneezes, the giggles at the solemn counting, the hiccup cure, the prank war, the practical joke, the parrot that repeats things, the goat that eats a ribbon, the swapped sandals, the identical outfits, the treacherous banana that lands upright on a log and the mysterious noise at the meeting (the rulebook's own examples; HUM-01 and HUM-39 take those ideas in different directions), the toddler's nonsense word, and the WD animals-in-odd-places set.
