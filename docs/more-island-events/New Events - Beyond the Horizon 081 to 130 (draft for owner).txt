NEW EVENTS — BEYOND THE HORIZON, EVENTS 081 to 130 (draft for the owner, 2026-11-15)
Fifty more speculative branching events continuing "Isola — Beyond the Horizon" (001-080), in the same format: EVENT nnn — TITLE, SETUP, choices A/B/C, two mutually exclusive outcomes per choice, and an optional "Content note:".
Everything said here about the outside world is a guess, a story, an analogy or a disagreement, never a confirmation. No outside place is named. The villagers keep asking whether distant people might live as they do, as often as they imagine how different they might be.
All five games unless noted. Story only: no new sprites, NPCs, structures or mechanics. Edit anything and send it back; nothing ships until you approve.

ALL OUTSIDE-WORLD SCENARIOS REMAIN SPECULATION. No distant people,

Rewards, where given, are small and game-supported, or none.


EVENT 081 — DO THEY SEE THE SAME MOON?
SETUP: On a clear night, a child asks whether people on the far side of the sea are looking at the same moon right now.
A. Imagine the Same Moon.
 A1. An elder says that if anyone is out there, the moon would be the one thing everyone shares; the child waves at it, just in case.
 A2. A navigator points out that it might be daytime somewhere else, and the child is astonished that the moon could be "taking turns."
B. Imagine a Different Sky.
 B1. A scholar wonders whether distant lands might see different stars; the idea makes the night feel larger.
 B2. A skeptic says there is no way to know, and the child decides to keep both ideas.
C. Ask What They Might Feel Looking Up.
 C1. A parent imagines a distant parent pointing at the moon for a sleepy child; the village falls quiet.
 C2. A fisher says perhaps some people are too busy to look up at all, and everyone laughs because they know someone like that.

EVENT 082 — THE GAME WATCHED BY A THOUSAND
SETUP: After a lively ball game on the beach, a teenager wonders whether distant people might gather in huge crowds just to watch others play.
A. Imagine Enormous Crowds.
 A1. The players imagine a crowd so loud that no one could hear the score; they cheer each other twice as loudly in practice.
 A2. A quiet villager says they would find such noise exhausting, and nobody argues.
B. Imagine Games Played for Fun Alone.
 B1. A child suggests that distant children might play just as they do, with made-up rules and arguments.
 B2. An elder wonders whether games far away could become so serious that the fun goes out of them.
C. Ask Who Gets to Play.
 C1. The players wonder whether everyone far away would get a turn, or only the best; they decide everyone gets a turn here.
 C2. A villager who uses a wheelchair imagines games built for every kind of body; the others start designing one immediately.

 C1. A child refuses to believe anywhere could be cold all the time; an elder says they might be right.
 C2. A navigator mentions sailors' stories of cold winds, while admitting stories grow in the telling.

 C2. A child says, "Just call us by our names," and the discussion ends there.
