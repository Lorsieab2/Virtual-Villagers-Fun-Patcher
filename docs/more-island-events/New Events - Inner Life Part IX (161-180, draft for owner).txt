(picks two or more children aged 7-12)

At the edge of the jungle there's an old tree with a hollow in the trunk and roots like twisted fingers. All the children agree it's scary, though none of them can say why. Today the others are daring each other to touch it. It's (name1)'s turn.

Touch the Tree:
(name1) walks up to the tree very slowly. (His/her) friends are all holding their breath. (He/she) reaches out and touches the bark. It's warm, and rough, and quite ordinary. A beetle crawls out of the hollow, and every single child screams and runs all the way back to the village, laughing. Even (name1).

178. The Tallest Sandcastle
(picks two or more children aged 4-12)

(name1) and the other children are building the tallest sandcastle in the history of Isola. It has towers. It has a moat. It has a seaweed flag. It has taken all afternoon. The tide is coming in.

Build a Wall to Hold Back the Sea:
The children dig frantically, piling up a wall of sand. The first wave stops at the wall. The children cheer. The second wave goes round the side. The third wave goes over the top. The castle slowly slumps into the sea, the seaweed flag last of all. The children watch it go, then immediately start building another one higher up the beach.

Let the Sea Have It:
"It's the sea's turn now," says (name1), sitting back. The children sit in a row and watch the waves come in, a little bit at a time, until the towers melt and the moat fills and the whole castle is a smooth little lump. It's sad, and beautiful, and somehow very funny. Somebody suggests they do it all again tomorrow.
