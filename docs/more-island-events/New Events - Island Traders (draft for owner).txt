NEW EVENTS - ISLAND TRADERS (TRD-01..13, draft for the owner, 2026-11-18)
A trader's canoe comes ashore with something to offer and something to ask for. Trade, haggle (it may go better or worse), or send the trader on with thanks.
Written in the style of the owner's own events. Edit anything and send it back; nothing ships until you approve.
(name1) fills in with a villager's name; (he/she), (him/her), (his/her) follow the villager. Each trader's own name is part of the story.
All Isola rules apply. Each [effect: ...] really changes the village's stores, never below 0; an offer is made only when the village has what it would give. Without enough, the trader moves on.

TRD-01. The Salt Trader
(picks an adult)

A canoe slides onto the beach at low tide, so heavy with clay jars that it sits low in the water. Its owner is Tavi, a salt trader with sun-cracked hands and a laugh you can hear from the huts. (name1) is the first to reach the shore. Tavi lifts the lid of one jar to show white salt, ground fine, and says it will keep fish for a whole season. In return Tavi asks for a share of the village's food for the long paddle home. (name1) has to decide what the village can spare.

Trade:
(name1) carries baskets down to the canoe, and Tavi fills a row of the village's pots with salt, telling (him/her) how to pack fish in it layer by layer. That evening the cooks try it on the catch and nobody can stop talking about it. [effect: trade 150 food for 400 tech points]

Haggle:
(name1) points out, politely, that the paddle home is downwind. Tavi laughs so hard that a jar nearly slips, then adds two more jars to the deal "for the nerve of it." The cooks are still using that salt long after the canoe has gone. [effect: trade 150 food for 600 tech points]
(name1) pushes a little too hard, and Tavi's smile thins. In the end Tavi trades, but only half a jar, sealed with a sniff. (name1) walks back up the beach knowing the village paid full price for a lesson in manners. [effect: trade 150 food for 250 tech points]

Decline:
(name1) thanks Tavi and explains that the stores are needed at home this season. Tavi shrugs, shares a handful of salt anyway "so you remember the taste," and is gone with the next tide.
Tavi looks over the village's stores, sees there is little to spare, and paddles on with a friendly wave.

TRD-02. The Star-Path Keeper
(picks an adult)

An old navigator named Hoku comes ashore to wait out a storm. Hoku has crossed more open water than anyone on Isola has ever seen, steering by the stars, and offers to teach someone the star paths in exchange for food for the next crossing. (name1) sits with Hoku by the fire as the rain comes down and has to decide whether the lessons are worth the baskets.

Trade:
For three nights, (name1) lies on the sand beside Hoku, learning the names of the bright stars and the paths between them. When the skies clear, Hoku leaves with the baskets, and (name1) is already drawing the star paths in the sand for anyone who will listen. [effect: trade 200 food for 500 tech points]

Haggle:
(name1) offers to help mend the canoe's lashings as part of the price. Hoku is so pleased with the work that the lessons run late every night, until (name1) knows more star paths than (he/she) can remember. [effect: trade 200 food for 750 tech points]
(name1) bargains so long that the storm passes before they agree. Hoku has time for only one night of lessons before the tide turns, and most of it is spent on a single, very bright star. [effect: trade 200 food for 300 tech points]

Decline:
(name1) gives Hoku a dry place to sleep and a warm meal, and no more. Hoku leaves at dawn, pointing once at the fading morning star as a parting gift.
Hoku sees how thin the village's stores are, refuses to take any food at all, and leaves at dawn with a wave.

TRD-03. The Bush Mender (VV1 only)
(picks an adult)

A wiry traveler named Mele arrives with a bundle of cuttings wrapped in wet leaves. Mele says the berry bushes on this island are tired, and that grafting a few strong cuttings onto them will make them heavy with berries again. Mele asks for tech in return: the village's learning, shared over the fire. (name1) looks at the bushes, then at the cuttings, and has to decide.

Trade:
Mele and (name1) spend a whole day bent over the bushes, binding cuttings with strips of bark. Within weeks the branches are bowed with berries, and Mele has gone, carrying everything (name1) could teach about the island. [effect: trade 400 tech points for 900 berries]

Haggle:
(name1) bargains warmly and well. Mele, charmed, grafts every bush on the hill instead of just a few, and the berries come in so thick the children eat themselves sleepy. [effect: trade 400 tech points for 1400 berries]
(name1) haggles until Mele grows impatient and grafts only the bushes nearest the path. They do fruit, eventually, and the rest of the hill looks on, a little tired. [effect: trade 400 tech points for 500 berries]

Decline:
(name1) decides the bushes will recover on their own. Mele leaves a single cutting in the ground anyway, "for luck," and walks on along the shore.
Mele sees the village has little to share, gives (name1) a few tips about pruning for free, and walks on along the shore.

TRD-04. The Net Weaver (VV2 only)
(picks an adult)

A woman named Rua comes ashore with a canoe full of nets and a basket of tiny live fish swimming in seawater. Rua says the village's cove has been overfished, and that the young fish in her basket, set free in the shallows, will grow and fill the waters again. She asks for food in return. (name1) watches the little fish dart around the basket and has to decide.

Trade:
(name1) wades out with Rua and tips the basket into the shallows. The tiny fish scatter like thrown pebbles. Months later the fishers swear the cove has never been so full. [effect: trade 150 food for 400 fish]

Haggle:
(name1) offers to help Rua repair a torn net while they talk. Rua is so grateful that she empties a second basket into the cove as well, and the shallows flicker silver for days. [effect: trade 150 food for 650 fish]
(name1) haggles hard, and Rua only lets half the basket go, keeping the rest for the next island on her way. The cove fills slowly. [effect: trade 150 food for 200 fish]

Decline:
(name1) decides the cove will recover in its own time. Rua nods, and releases a handful of the little fish anyway before she goes. "For the cove," she says.
Rua sees the village has little food to spare, lets a handful of little fish go in the shallows as a gift, and paddles on.

TRD-07. The Fruit Grafter (VV3 only)
(picks an adult)

A quiet old man named Pono comes up the path carrying a knife, a roll of bark twine and nothing else. He says he grafts fruit trees for a living, and that one afternoon's work will make the village's trees heavy with fruit. He asks only to be taught something new in return. (name1) and Pono stand under the trees, and (name1) has to decide.

Trade:
Pono climbs like a much younger man, binding new wood to old. Before long the trees are heavy with fruit, and Pono leaves with everything (name1) could teach him. [effect: trade 400 tech points for 400 fruit]

Haggle:
(name1) offers to climb the high branches so Pono need not. Pono, delighted, grafts every tree twice over, and the fruit comes in so thick the branches have to be propped up. [effect: trade 400 tech points for 700 fruit]
(name1) haggles, and Pono, who hates haggling, grafts only the lowest branches before he goes home. The fruit comes, but only within easy reach. [effect: trade 400 tech points for 200 fruit]

Decline:
(name1) thanks Pono and says the trees will do as they are. Pono pats the nearest trunk, as if apologizing to it, and leaves.
Pono sees the trees have nowhere for new fruit to go, and leaves with a nod and nothing taken.

NOTES FOR THE OWNER
- Every trade is all or nothing: the village gives and gets exactly what the [effect: ...] says, or nothing at all. An offer is made only when the village has what it would give; without it, the event shows its last line instead (the trader moves on). Nothing ever goes below 0, and a gain stops at the game's own ceiling.
- Haggle is a coin toss: the better or the worse of the two Haggle lines, equally likely.
- Collectibles: a trader only ever takes a spare (a piece found twice), never the last one; The Lost Children keeps no spares, so there a trader only gives.
- Not traded, because the game keeps no count (words only): mushrooms; The Secret City's and New Believers' fish and crabs; The Tree of Life's fish, Golden Fish, crabs and hot rocks; potions, herbs, stews, firewood, dry grass and water.
- Yellow fruit (The Tree of Life): kept inside the Yellow Fruit Trees patch, which has no way yet for an event to give or take fruit. Not offered until it does.
