NEW EVENTS - LIVING CULTURE IX: REPAIR, FAIRNESS AND MANY FUTURES (LCI-01..50, draft, 2026-11-18)
From the owner's Volume V concepts (401-500), the 30 themes and the five deeper subjects: old wrongs and their repair, who is heard, prejudice and fairness, care for minds and bodies, animals and hard tradeoffs, inventions and bad inventions, family life, and an island that keeps choosing its own future.
Written in the style of the owner's own events. The owner waived the review ("I'm sure the draft events are fine"); every event passed the Isola rules and the final 15-point review, logged in the notes.
(name1) to (name4) fill in with villagers' names; (he/she), (him/her), (his/her) follow the villager.
All Isola rules apply. All five games unless noted. Old wrongs are only implied and belong to the past; nobody is blamed for what their ancestors did. A [Solidarity: raise/lower/neutral] tag marks how an ending feels for the village (a planned counter; no effect yet).

LCI-01. The Land That Was Taken
(picks two adults)

(name1) has learned from an old elder that the garden on the sunny slope belonged to (his/her) family, until it was taken generations ago in an unfair trade. For forty years, (name2)'s family has farmed it, and it is the only garden they have ever known. Neither family did anything wrong. What should the village do?

Share the Garden:

The two families agree to farm the slope together. It is awkward at first: two ways of planting, two ways of weeding. By the third season, the children of both families are running between the rows as if it had always been one garden. [effect: small Farming skill for (name1)] [Solidarity: raise]

Another Form of Repair:

The village gives (name1)'s family a new garden on the hill and carves their old family mark on a stone at the edge of the sunny slope. (name1) is grateful, and also not satisfied. Some things cannot be fully repaired. The stone stays. [Solidarity: neutral]

Return It to the Family:

The staff goes to (name1)'s family. (name1) holds it for a long time without saying anything. The family invites the village to see it once a year, at the harvest. Everyone comes. [Solidarity: raise]

Keep It in the Meeting Hut:

(name1)'s family agrees, after a long discussion, to let it hang in the meeting hut, with the family's name carved beside it. (name1) visits it every morning. Some of (his/her) relatives still wish it were home. [effect: small Research skill for (name2)] [Solidarity: neutral]

LCI-04. The Forgotten Builders
(picks a researcher and an elder)

Add Their Names:

The workers' names are found, as many as can be, and carved on the wall. Their descendants come to touch them. The old song gets a new verse. (name2) sings it, gruffly. [effect: small tech gain] [Solidarity: raise]

Tell the Whole Truth Too:

(name1) tells the whole story at the gathering: the workers, and the leader who took the credit. Some villagers are upset to have their hero lessened. Others say it was long overdue. The names are carved, and the argument goes on. [effect: small tech gain] [Solidarity: neutral]

Give Them a Seat:

LCI-06. The Treaty of Two Shores
(picks two adults)

The village and the settlement across the bay both say the northern fishing ground is theirs. (name1) wants to share it by season: one village in the wet season, one in the dry. (name2) wants to divide it down the middle with a line of floats. What should the treaty say?

Share by Season:

The treaty is carved on two posts, one on each shore. The first season goes well. The second, a storm keeps the other village home for weeks, and they ask for extra time. The village agrees. The treaty bends, and holds. [effect: small food gain] [Solidarity: raise]

Divide It in Two:

The floats are laid. The fish do not respect them at all. Within a month, the fishers of both villages are arguing over which side a fish was caught on. They switch to sharing by season. [Solidarity: neutral]

LCI-07. The Burden of Leadership
(picks an adult and an elder)

At the gathering, the chief stood up and said, quietly, that (he/she) is exhausted: every dispute, every storm, every hungry family comes to (his/her) door. (name1) is shocked. (name2) says leaders were never meant to carry everything alone. What should the village do?

The village chooses helpers: one for disputes, one for the stores, one for the canoes. The chief keeps the hardest decisions. (He/she) sleeps through the night for the first time in a year, and says so at the next gathering, to cheers. [effect: small Research skill for (name1)] [Solidarity: raise]

Let the Chief Rest:

The village asks (name2) to lead for a moon while the chief rests. The chief comes back rested, and also realizes the village did well without (him/her). That is a relief and a strange feeling, both. [Solidarity: neutral]

(name1) asks everyone what they actually want. It turns out almost everyone wants more fish, closer to home. The pond is moved, made smaller and built with everyone's help. Not everyone is happy. Most are. [effect: small Research skill for (name1)] [Solidarity: raise]

The assembly ends in anger. People go home grumbling. The pond is not built. (name2) says the village will try again when tempers cool. Some people say it was a failure. (name1) says it was a beginning. [Solidarity: lower]

(picks two adults)

A new water channel would bring water to twenty gardens. The only straight path runs through the gardens of (name2)'s family and one other. (name1) says the many outweigh the few. (name2) says (his/her) grandmother planted that garden. What should the village decide?

The channel winds around the two gardens, which makes it twice as long and takes a whole season more. The other families grumble about the wait. (name2)'s grandmother's garden stays where it is. [Solidarity: raise]

The village keeps trading the old way, between families. It works. It is slower. (name1) keeps suggesting a market. Eventually, (name2) agrees to try one, small. [Solidarity: neutral]

LCI-13. The Ceremony Reimagined
(picks an elder and an adult)

The harvest ceremony has always used red feathers, but the red birds have become rare, and there are hardly any feathers left. (name2) suggests using red flowers instead. (name1) says flowers would change the meaning. What should the village do?

The ceremony is held with red flowers. Some elders refuse to take part. Most people find it beautiful. Over the years, the flowers become part of the meaning, and the red birds, left alone, slowly come back. [effect: small Farming skill for (name2)] [Solidarity: neutral]

Wait for the Feathers:

The village skips the ceremony for a year, and leaves the red birds in peace. It feels strange and empty. The next year, a few feathers are found, and the ceremony is held, smaller and more precious than before. [Solidarity: neutral]

(name2) says the village will hold it once and see. It is held, and loved, and held again the next year, and the next. By the fifth year, (name2) admits it has become old enough. [effect: everyone celebrates] [Solidarity: raise]

(name1) tries every step on the boards, over and over. The first cloth falls apart. The tenth holds. (name1) sits with (name2) and the other elders, who remember pieces: the beating sound, the soaking in the stream, the song that kept time. Together with the boards, it is enough. The craft comes back, with its song. [effect: small Building skill for (name1)] [Solidarity: raise]

(picks two adults)

The village makes the feast open to anyone who wants to come, without discussing the old custom. (name1)'s family comes, a little stiffly. Some people move away from them. Next year, fewer people do. [Solidarity: neutral]
