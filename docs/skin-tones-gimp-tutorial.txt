How to mark skin in GIMP (VVFP Skin Tones Workbench)
=====================================================

The "Skin mask" layer decides which pixels get a skin tone, in every tone at once.
White = skin. Empty (see-through) = not skin (hair, eyes, teeth, clothes, outlines...).
Fix the mask once and all ten tones follow it.

This guide is for GIMP 3. Keys in [brackets].


1. OPEN A FILE
   - File > Open [Ctrl+O], go to this folder, open the game's folder (vv1 ... vv5) and pick a .xcf,
     e.g. vv1\vv1_male_heads.xcf.
   - If GIMP asks about a colour profile, choose "Keep".

2. SHOW THE LAYERS PANEL
   - Windows > Dockable Dialogs > Layers [Ctrl+L] if it is not already showing.
   - You will see, top to bottom:
       Hair and non-skin (vanilla)   (locked)
       Tone 10 - S10 Ebony ... Tone 01 - S1 Porcelain
       Skin mask                     (hidden)
       Game's own art                (locked)

3. SET UP THE VIEW SO YOU CAN SEE WHAT YOU'RE MARKING
   - Click the EYE next to "Skin mask" to show it.
   - Click the NAME "Skin mask" so it is the selected layer (highlighted). Painting always goes
     on the selected layer, so check this every time.
   - Click the eye next to "Hair and non-skin (vanilla)" to HIDE it while you mark, so it doesn't
     cover the mask.
   - Lower "Skin mask"'s Opacity slider (top of the Layers panel) to about 50, so you can see the
     game's art through the white. (Put it back to 100 before saving: step 7.)
   - Zoom in: press [4] for 800% or [5] for 1600%. [1] goes back to 100%.
     Hold [Space] and move the mouse to pan around.

4. ADD SKIN (paint white)
   - Press [D] to reset the colours (black and white), then [X] if needed so the FOREGROUND
     (top-left square in the toolbox) is WHITE.
   - Pick the Pencil tool [N]. In Tool Options set Size to 1 (one pixel), or bigger for big areas.
     Use the Pencil, not the Paintbrush: the Pencil gives hard, exact pixels.
   - Click or drag over every pixel of skin that is missing: necks, ears, arms, hands, legs,
     feet, the strip of skin between hair and clothes.

5. REMOVE SKIN (erase)
   - Pick the Eraser [Shift+E]. In Tool Options tick "Hard edge" and set Size to 1.
   - Erase pixels that are NOT skin but got marked: pale blond hair, eye whites, teeth,
     cream/tan/yellow clothes, belts, jewellery.
   - If the eraser paints a colour instead of making pixels see-through, the layer has no
     transparency: Layer > Transparency > Add Alpha Channel, then erase again.

   Big areas faster (optional):
   - Free Select tool [F]: click around an area to outline it, double-click to close.
   - With "Skin mask" selected: Edit > Fill with FG Color [Ctrl+,] to mark it all as skin,
     or Edit > Clear [Delete] to unmark it all. Select > None [Ctrl+Shift+A] when done.

6. CHECK YOUR WORK
   - Hide "Skin mask" (its eye), and show a DARK tone, e.g. "Tone 10 - S10 Ebony" (click its eye;
     hide the other tone layers), and show "Hair and non-skin (vanilla)" again.
   - Any light, untoned patch of skin now stands out on the dark tone: that's skin you still
     need to add (step 4). Any hair or clothing turned dark brown: that's skin you need to
     remove (step 5).
   - Note: the tone layers in the .xcf are pictures made when the file was built. They do NOT change
     when you change the mask, in GIMP or after "import". "import" makes the mod use your mask (every
     tone you did not paint yourself is made again from it); the .xcf you saved stays exactly yours.
     So judge a mask change with the original art and the mask view. To get a file whose tone layers
     show your mask, see "TO SEE YOUR MASK IN THE TONE LAYERS" below.

7. SAVE
   - Set "Skin mask" Opacity back to 100. (If you forget, nothing is lost: "import" reads the mask
     the same at any opacity, and whether or not a layer's eye is on.)
   - Leave layer names, order and the picture size exactly as they are (don't rename, merge,
     delete, crop or scale).
   - File > Save [Ctrl+S]. Keep it as .xcf in the same place with the same name.
     (Don't use File > Export.)

8. BRING YOUR EDITS INTO THE MOD
   In the patcher's folder, run:
       python scripts\skin_tone_sheets.py import --game vv1        (vv1 ... vv5)
   Your mask (and any tone layer you painted yourself) is kept in
       <this folder>\<game>\edits\<sheet>\
   and wins over the automatic version from then on. Rebuilding the workbench never replaces
   a file you have changed.

TO SEE YOUR MASK IN THE TONE LAYERS (optional)
   After "import", your edits are safe in the "edits" folder. If you then want a fresh .xcf whose
   ten tone layers are made from your mask: delete that one .xcf yourself and run
       python scripts\skin_tone_sheets.py workbench --game vv1 --images "<the game's Images folder>"
   The new file has your mask and your own painted tone layers in it. Only delete a file after
   "import" has kept its edits.

TIPS
   - Do one sheet at a time and save often.
   - A villager's skin is the same on every frame of a sheet, so work frame by frame across the row.
   - If you paint directly on a tone layer (to touch up one tone), also paint the Skin mask white
     under it: only paint inside the mask is used.
   - Made a mistake? Edit > Undo [Ctrl+Z], as many times as you need.
